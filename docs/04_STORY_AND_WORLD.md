# 04 — Story & World Bible

## Logline
*1936. A self-taught cartographer follows her missing mentor's half-burned map into five forgotten ruins. She is
racing a secret society to reassemble the **Meridian Engine**, a machine that the ancients built to "hold the
world still."*

## Tone
Pulp adventure in the style of Saturday-matinee serials: warm, witty and brave, with real awe at the ruins.
Danger is cartoon-lethal (crushed, then respawn) and never gory. Every place had people who lived there, and
the game respects them. The real villain is greed, not the cultures whose ruins you explore.

## Characters
**Nadia Varga** (player character): 27, a self-taught mapmaker from Istanbul's harbour district. Quick-witted,
stubborn, scared of deep water and goes in anyway. Signature gear: a satchel, a grapple-whip made from survey
cord, and a brass lantern. She **draws maps** while others dig for gold. That is why the journal mechanic exists.

**Professor Elias Thorne** (mentor, missing): an archaeologist who taught Nadia to read old glyphs. He vanished
after finding the first Sunstone. His burned notes are the trail you follow, and they are the game's hint system.

**Baron Ostrander Vael** (antagonist): a charming industrialist who leads the **Gilded Compass**, a society of
collectors. He believes the Engine can "stop" the world's turning towards war, and will freeze history so that
his own empire is on top. His agents race you through every world.

**Tamsin "Tam" Reyes** (ally / rival): a pilot and smuggler who flies Nadia between sites. She's in it for the
money, until World 4.

**Kiko** (companion): a capuchin monkey who stays in camp. He's the face of the hub screen and occasionally
fetches secret gems. (He's cosmetic, not a mechanic in v1.)

## The Meridian Engine (the myth)
An ancient, forgotten "Meridian" culture spread across the world's trade routes. They built a machine to calm
earthquakes by "holding" the earth's rhythm. It worked too well: time stalled in the cities around it, and they
emptied. The builders split it into **five Sunstones** and hid each one in a guardian temple, built in
partnership with a local civilisation. Each temple uses one element as its lock.

## Act structure
**Prologue — "The Burned Map"** (tutorial, Istanbul cistern): Nadia searches Thorne's flooded study and learns
to move, push and collect. The Gilded Compass breaks in, and she escapes through the cistern with a page of the map.

**World 1 — The Jade Cenote** (Yucatán-inspired jungle sinkhole). *Element: WATER.* Flooding chambers, floating
crates, water raising and lowering platforms. Boss: **the Stone Jaguar**, a statue guardian who charges in straight
lines. Bait it into falling boulders. Finding: Thorne was here, and alive.

**World 2 — The Necropolis of Qadir** (Saharan desert tomb city). *Element: SAND.* Sand flows like a liquid
through grates, quicksand, and mirrors that redirect light beams to open doors. Boss: **the Scarab Colossus**,
which burrows and emerges beneath boulders. Twist: Tam sold your location to Vael.

**World 3 — The Barrow of Hrimfjall** (Norse glacier tomb). *Element: ICE.* Sliding tiles (you and boulders slide
until you hit something), freezing water into bridges, thin ice. Boss: **the Draugr Jarl**, who freezes the
arena in waves. Finding: Thorne joined Vael willingly, or so the notes claim.

**World 4 — The Ember Forge of Sahyadri** (Deccan volcanic forge). *Element: LAVA/HEAT.* Lava flows that harden
when they meet water, heat tiles, and molten-gold sliders. Boss: **the Brass Naga**, an automaton in a lava ring.
Tam returns and chooses your side. Thorne is revealed as Vael's prisoner, forced to translate.

**World 5 — The Meridian Core** (a sunken clockwork city in the Atlantic shelf). *Element: GEARS/TIME.* Rotating
room sections, a clock-tick rhythm where some tiles move only on the beat, and frozen-time zones where falling
objects hang in mid-air. Final boss: **Vael in the Engine Throne**, a multi-phase puzzle fight. You rotate the
arena to drop the Sunstones out of the machine.

**Epilogue:** Nadia chooses to scatter the Sunstones again rather than use them. Thorne retires and gives her his
journal. Post-credits: a sixth map page shows "the place where the Meridians came from" (a hook for the DLC).

## World structure (per world)
- 12 levels: 3 that teach the element, 6 that combine it with other mechanics, 2 that remix it, and 1 chase
  level (an auto-scrolling collapse escape).
- 1 boss arena.
- 3 **Lost Relic** secrets per world. Find all 15 to unlock the true ending scene and the "Atlas" bonus world.

## Journal (narrative delivery)
- **Map pages:** draw themselves as you explore. Unexplored rooms show "?" marks.
- **Thorne's notes:** one per level, partially burned. Each is a hint for that level's hardest secret.
- **Glyph tablets:** collectibles that translate into the Meridian backstory. This is the lore feed.
- Cutscenes: short animated scenes in the game engine (≤ 45 s, can be skipped) at the start and end of each world.

## Naming & sensitivity rules
- Settings are *inspired by* real regions and are fictionalised: no real sacred sites by name, and no cultures
  shown as "savage" or "cursed."
- Each world has a short *Field Notes* page about the real-world culture it is inspired by, written respectfully
  and reviewed by a consultant before launch.
